**Scope:** Internal consistency only — structural-dimension coverage, AD enforceability, AD↔diagram/table/AC/capability-map/Deferred alignment, CAP-1..CAP-24 coverage, baseline/Deferred contradictions, and obsolete multi-writer/multi-tenant machinery leakage.

## Verdict

**Conditional pass — one Blocking-class finding must be closed before this spine can be claimed AD-28-conformant; the document is otherwise internally mature and consistent.**

The spine covers all expected structural dimensions (paradigm, diagrams, invariants/rules, CRD ownership, durable-data ownership, dual-mode topology, consistency conventions, stack, packaging, trust boundaries, backup/recovery, scheduling, export/import, local-vs-k8s treatment, migration phases, ACs, capability map, assumptions, Deferred). CAP-1 through CAP-24 are all bound in the frontmatter and all appear in the Capability → Architecture Map. No obsolete multi-writer/multi-tenant machinery (e.g., `CodePlaneStorageShard`, `CodePlaneRepositoryIndex`, `CodePlaneSessionHandoff`, active/active controllers) leaks outside the "Future HA / multi-tenant profile" Deferred bullet — every baseline mention of these concepts is explicitly qualified as Deferred. However, the document fails its own AD-28 self-check for two ADs, and one AD references a CRD lifecycle class that does not exist in the closed CRD inventory it must be consistent with.

## Top Findings

1. **[High] AD-3 and AD-26 are never cited as "Governing decisions" in any Acceptance Criterion, violating AD-28's own rule.** AD-28 states "Every AD must govern at least one acceptance criterion and CI validates coverage." A full scan of the AC-1…AC-20 "Governing decisions" column (including expanded "AD-x through AD-y" ranges) shows every AD from AD-1 to AD-37 appears at least once **except AD-3** ("Agent execution is outside the Kubernetes control plane") and **AD-26** ("Migration is additive and conformance-gated"). Both only appear in their own section headers and in the Capability → Architecture Map, not in the AC table. As written, a CI job that enforces AD-28's coverage rule against the AC table would fail (or silently miss) these two ADs, which contradicts the document's own conformance-gating claim. Fix: add AD-3 and AD-26 to the "Governing decisions" of the relevant existing ACs (AD-3 clearly overlaps AC-3/AC-4/AC-5's worker-isolation content; AD-26 clearly overlaps the Migration and Compatibility Strategy table but has no AC today) or add a new AC for phased-migration conformance.

2. **[Medium] AD-15 references "operation" CRs as a garbage-collected CRD lifecycle class that does not exist in the closed CRD inventory.** AD-15 states: "Terminal attempt/operation/approval CRs archive then GC within 24 hours and retain at most three summaries of each kind per job." This implies three CRD kinds — attempt, **operation**, and approval — with archive/GC semantics. But AD-33's closed v1 namespaced inventory and the "CRD Ownership and Lifecycle Contract" table define only `CodePlaneJob`, `CodePlaneExecutionAttempt`, `CodePlaneApproval`, `CodePlaneRepositoryBinding`, `CodePlaneBackupEpoch`, and `CodePlaneImportSession` — there is no `CodePlaneOperation` (or similarly named) CRD. AD-11 in fact states the opposite: operations are tracked in "a durable SQLite operation ledger," not as Kubernetes objects. This leaves AD-15's "operation CRs" phrase inconsistent with AD-11 and AD-33/the CRD table — implementers and CI coverage tooling cannot tell whether a CRD kind is missing from the table or whether AD-15's wording is simply stale. Fix: either remove "operation" from AD-15's CRD GC sentence (if operations are SQLite-only, per AD-11) or add the missing CRD kind consistently to AD-33 and the CRD Ownership table.

3. **[Low] The `Binds:` CAP list on many ADs is broader than the reverse "Governed by" list in the Capability → Architecture Map (e.g., AD-34 binds CAP-15 but CAP-15's map row does not cite AD-34; AD-10 binds CAP-9 but CAP-9's map row does not cite AD-10).** This pattern recurs across roughly a third of the ADs, which suggests the capability map is an intentionally curated "primary owner" summary rather than an exhaustive mirror of every AD's `Binds:` field — plausibly by design given the density of this spine. Flagged as Low/informational rather than Blocking because it is systemic rather than an isolated slip, but it does mean the capability map cannot be relied on as a complete reverse-index of AD impact, which slightly weakens the rubric's "every AD... consistent with... capability map" guarantee. No single fix is required; if exhaustiveness is intended, the map would need a broader reconciliation pass rather than a spot fix.
